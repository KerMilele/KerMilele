## Professional Self-Assessment

### My Computer Science Journey

When I started my journey as a Computer Science student, my dream was to become a cybersecurity expert. At that time, Southern New Hampshire University did not offer cybersecurity as a major, so I chose Computer Science with the intention of specializing in cybersecurity later. When the university introduced the major in 2025, I was already progressing through my degree.

As I continued my studies, I became interested in how businesses use data to solve problems and make decisions. After doing my own research, I discovered data analytics and data science. This encouraged me to take Principles of Machine Learning (CS 407), where I learned how to analyze datasets, build predictive models, and evaluate their performance. I also developed an interest in web development, especially e-commerce applications. Python and React have become two of my strongest technical skills.

### Skills and Experiences

Throughout my program, I developed technical, communication, and teamwork skills through different projects. In my Machine Learning course, I collaborated with classmates to analyze data, compare models, and present our findings. This experience taught me how to share responsibilities, listen to different perspectives, and explain results clearly.

In CS 255 Systems Analysis and Design, I worked on the DriverPass project, where I analyzed business requirements and created system diagrams and documentation. This helped me understand how developers communicate technical solutions to stakeholders and design applications based on their needs.

I also gained experience with algorithms, databases, and software development. In CS 340, I used Python and MongoDB to build an interactive animal shelter dashboard with tables, charts, and a map. In my Computational Graphics and Visualization course, I created a 3D scene, which introduced me to computer graphics and visual design. These projects helped me understand different ways technology can organize, process, and present information.

Security was also an important part of my education. In my Software Security course, I learned about software vulnerabilities and the OWASP security risks. This helped me understand why developers must protect sensitive information, validate inputs, and consider security throughout the development process.

### My Capstone Project and ePortfolio

For my CS 499 capstone, I selected OWNHER, an e-commerce website I originally developed in April 2026 for my CS 465 Full Stack Development course. I chose this project because it allowed me to demonstrate software engineering, algorithms, databases, and security skills through one application.

My first enhancement focused on software engineering and design. I improved inventory management by adding stock information and validation to prevent customers from purchasing more products than available.

My second enhancement focused on algorithms and data structures. I improved product searching, filtering, and sorting to make the website easier to navigate.

My third enhancement focused on databases. I moved product and order information to MySQL, connected it to the Express backend, and added backend inventory validation and database security improvements.

Together, these enhancements show my ability to improve an existing application, solve problems, and connect different technologies. My code review and written narratives also demonstrate my ability to evaluate code and explain technical decisions.

### Professional Goals

Completing my Computer Science program has helped me discover my strengths and develop clearer career goals. I learned that software development requires more than programming. It also involves teamwork, communication, problem-solving, testing, and security.

My goal is to continue growing in data analytics, business intelligence, and data science while using my programming and web development skills. This ePortfolio represents my growth throughout the program and demonstrates my ability to apply what I have learned to real-world projects.
